# Chat

@mention the bot (or its role), or reply to one of its answers, and it answers in character. It also does what you ask, in plain English: "queue the strokes", "play the wall", "skip this", "play the airhorn", "say happy birthday in the GLaDOS voice". It needs an Anthropic API key on the host (see [Setting it up](#setting-it-up)).

## What it can do

- **[Music](music.md):** finds songs, albums and artists by name, on Spotify or YouTube Music as the server's [Music search](music.md#how-it-plays) setting picks, and adds them to the queue, starting the music in your voice channel if nothing is playing. An artist adds up to 10 of their best-known songs, and "their new album" looks up their albums by date, so it finds one newer than the model knows of. Asked about a name it doesn't know ("have you heard of Reality Awaits?"), it searches songs, albums and artists together before saying it isn't there. It also takes links, and skips, pauses, resumes, shuffles and turns Autoplay on or off. "Stop the music" pauses it; it only empties the queue when you ask for that. It can look at the queue ("what's next?", "did you shuffle it?"), and when you say what it just queued was wrong, it takes those songs out before adding the right ones. If an album went in shuffled and you'd like another order, it shuffles the queue again. It knows what's been played here before, so "play that Killers song from yesterday again" finds it.
- **[Sounds](soundboard.md):** plays a sound from the library in your voice channel, finding it by name.
- **[Speech](speech.md):** says something in your voice channel, in any voice and effect.
- **Pinging people:** "tell @Sam happy birthday" pings Sam. It can ping people talking in the channel lately, people you @mention, and people in voice; never anyone else, a role or @everyone.
- **Looking things up:** ask it about the server's games and it looks them up, with the same answers as the slash commands and the web UI:
  - what a group can play together, as [/whatdoweplay](games.md) picks from: the people in your voice channel, or whoever you name, co-op or PvP, Steam Deck, and games one purchase away;
  - one game: who owns it, needs it or wants it, whether it's on sale, and who has played it most;
  - [play stats](stats.md) for the server, a person, a game or two people compared, over the past week, month or year, or all time;
  - [deals](deals.md): what's on sale that the server plays or wants (or just what you want), bundles, free games, and the price of any game on Steam, with the cheapest official store selling its key and its lowest price ever;
  - songs played here before, newest or most played;
  - a game's latest patch notes, summed up;
  - how people are getting on with a game's [achievements](games.md), with linked Steam accounts;
  - what it knows about someone's gaming ("what do you know about me?"): their most played games here and on Steam, with hours, and how many of their Steam games they've never played.

  Anyone in the server can ask, as anyone can run those commands. When a name could be more than one person or game, it asks which.
- **[Game nights](getting-together.md#game-nights):** "set up a Valheim night friday at 9" posts the RSVP card here and makes the Discord event, as `/gamenight plan` does, with you as host; it says the day and time back, so a misread shows. "Make it weekly", "in the Gaming channel" and details work too. It lists what's planned and who's in, answers for you ("I'm in for friday", "can't make it"), and changes, cancels or stops repeating a night, which only its host or someone with Manage Events can.
- **[Votes and picks](games.md#what-do-we-play):** "start a vote on what to play" posts the `/gamevote` vote for your voice channel (only some games, if you name them, and for up to 10 minutes), and "just pick one" picks a random game the group can all play, as `/whatdoweplay` does; "reroll" picks another.
- **Catching you up:** "what did I miss?" sums up what was said in the channel since you last wrote there, and "catch me up on today" or "since yesterday" the last few hours: plans, decisions, and anything asked of or about you. It reads up to 3 days and 500 messages, only in that channel.
- **[Sounds from links](soundboard.md):** "make a sound called bonk from this, 0:05 to 0:12" with a YouTube, TikTok, Instagram Reel or SoundCloud link downloads it and adds that part to the soundboard, as **Add from a link** in the web UI does (without a start and end, the whole thing if it's 30 seconds or less). It answers at once and posts how it went when it's done. Only for people who can change sounds, and only with the [music container](self-hosting.md#the-music-container); replacing a sound is in the web UI.
- **Reminders:** "remind me in 20 minutes to check the oven", "remind Sam and me friday at 8pm about the raid". A time without am or pm, like "at 8", it asks about. It's posted in the channel you asked in, pinging only the people it's for (up to 5, never a role or everyone), up to 30 days ahead, and it says the time back. Asked again for the same thing at about the same time, it doesn't set it twice. "What reminders do I have?" lists yours, and you can cancel one you set, or take yourself off one someone set for you. Each person can have 10 waiting. If the bot is down when one is due, it's posted when it's back, up to an hour late. The web UI's Settings page lists them all under **Chat**, with **Cancel**.
- **Searching the web:** for a game's strategy, builds, guides, tips or wiki facts ("what's a good Ironclad build in Slay the Spire 2?", "how do I beat Bonemass?"), and news or anything else newer than the model knows, it searches the web, answers from what it found, and links its sources in small text under the answer (not when it only says the answer in voice). It usually searches once, and takes about 4 seconds. **Search the web** in Settings turns it off.
- **Pointing you to commands:** for what it can't do itself, like adding a game to the library, it tells you the command to use, and you can click it. Asked how to use the [web UI](web-ui.md), it says what each tab is for, with a link once the host sets `WEB_ADDRESS`.
- **Roasting rude people:** be rude to it ("you useless pile of scrap") and it looks up your games and hits back with an insult, as harsh as its [profile](#profiles) says, about something different each time: one of the games you've put the most hours into, here or on Steam, what you've played lately, one you've barely touched or bought and never played, or your backlog, with a jab only its players would get. If nothing comes to mind, it looks up the easy achievements you still don't have. "Roast Sam" does the same to Sam, said to Sam alone, with no mention of who asked. It stays on gaming, taste and what was said here: never looks, family, race, religion, gender, sexuality or health. Swearing about a game isn't rudeness to it, and if a rude message also asks for something, it does that too. **Roast rude people** in [Settings](#settings) turns it off.
- **Remembering you:** tell it something to remember about you, like what to call you, and it keeps a short note (up to 200 characters) that it reads whenever you ask it something. Only about the person asking. The web UI's **What chat remembers** card lists the notes, with a button to forget each.

It does these as you: only what you could do yourself. With a DJ role set, people without it can't control the music through it either, the text to speech role and cooldown still apply, and sounds and speech need you in a voice channel. Activity shows what you asked, as "Asked in chat", and what it did, under your name. A question gets an answer: "do you know their new album?" doesn't queue it.

Anything else, it just answers, as its [profile](#profiles) says: by default in a few sentences at most, with real opinions given straight, swearing when that fits, and nothing but contempt for fascists and Nazis. It can't edit or delete messages or change settings. It sees the channel's last 20 messages, images attached to the message asking it or the one it replies to (up to 3, each under 5 MB), so "rate my loadout" or "what does this say?" work, the date and time, what's playing, whether you're in voice, who's in the server's voice channels and what games people are playing, so it can follow along. Voice channels only some people can see aren't mentioned. Past that it remembers only the notes above, and it doesn't read messages that don't @mention it or reply to one of its answers. Replies to its other posts, like hop-on pings and recaps, are left to people.

## Trying it in the web UI

The web UI's **Chat** tab asks the bot as you would in Discord and shows the answer there, so a personality, a roast or a request can be tried without anyone seeing it. Pick who to ask as (you, once the page knows who you are) and the channel it's asked in, for "what did I miss": one everyone in the server can see, so asking as someone else shows nothing private. **Profile** answers as another profile than the server's, with its personality, name and voice, without picking it, until the browser tab is closed or a profile is picked with **Use**. Type as in Discord: an @ opens a list of the server's members, by their name here, display name or username, to pick who to mention. The conversation stays in the browser tab until **Clear**, and the bot reads it as the channel's last messages.

- **It's the real answer:** the same instructions, personality, model and lookups as in Discord. It costs the same, counts toward the day's answers and has the same 10-second wait.
- **Nothing it does happens:** under each answer is what it would have done, like queueing music, planning a game night or remembering something, and who it would have pinged. The model is told each was done, so it answers as it would have. Nothing is posted, logged in Activity or remembered.
- **What it would say aloud plays in your browser:** the answer, in its profile's voice and effect, when the server's **Answer in voice** setting has answers said and **Say answers aloud** is on, plus any line it would say in another voice and any sound it would play. **Hear it** plays them again. Above the conversation it says how the answer goes out in Discord.

## Settings

The **Chat** card in the web UI's Settings has them all, and says how many answers it gave this month and roughly what they cost:

- **Answer in chat:** on or off for the server.
- **Profile:** who the bot is, from the [profiles](#profiles) below. It starts as the Robot butler.
- **Search the web:** on by default; see [Privacy and cost](#privacy-and-cost).
- **Lines on automatic posts:** on by default. The bot's own posts (hop-on pings, session and monthly recaps, the weekly leaderboard, sales, bundles, free games and patch notes) get a line in its personality above them, written by the chat model from what the post says. Each is a small fraction of a cent, counts toward the month's cost but not the daily answer limit, and a post goes out without its line if the model is slow or down.
- **Roast rude people:** on by default; how harsh it is is set in the profile. Off, it takes rudeness in its stride, in character.
- **Take actions:** on by default. Off, it doesn't plan, change or answer game nights, start votes, set reminders or make sounds: it points people to the game night and vote commands, and says it can't set reminders. It still lists game nights and picks games, and reminders already set are still posted (and listed in the web UI, to cancel).
- **Model:** **Claude Haiku 5.5**, the default and the quickest (about 2 seconds for a typical answer); **Claude Sonnet 5.5**, which follows messy requests better, at about 20 times the cost and about 3 seconds; or **Claude Opus 5.5**, the smartest, at about 40 times the cost and about 4 seconds.
- **Answer in voice:** says each answer in its profile's voice when the person asking is in a voice channel and can use speech. **Text and voice** posts it too; **Voice only** just reacts 🔊, and remembers what it said so it can follow on; an answer that pings someone is posted too, as pings only work in text. What it says is in Activity as said by the bot. It's posted instead when it can't be said: they aren't in voice, it's over the longest line, the bot is busy in another voice channel, or it has just said something for them. Off by default.
- **Answers a day:** 200 by default, in the server's time zone. After that it says once that it's done for the day. Each person also waits 10 seconds between answers; asking sooner gets a ⏳.

## Profiles

A profile is who the bot is: a personality, how it talks, what it's called, its photo and the voice it says its own lines in. The **Profiles** card under Chat in Settings lists them, with **Use**, **Try**, **Edit**, **Copy** and **Delete**, and **New profile** makes one. Every server on the bot shares them, and each uses the one picked in its **Profile** setting, so switching between, say, a Yoda and a Bender changes how it talks, what it's called and how it sounds at once. **Try** opens the [Chat tab](#trying-it-in-the-web-ui) as that profile, to hear it before anyone else does. Activity shows each one made, changed or deleted, and each switch.

The profile's face sits in the web UI's header, left of the server, on every tab: its photo, or its initials on a colour of its own without one. Hovering says its name, and a click or tap opens a menu of the profiles to pick another for the server, as **Use** does, with **Edit profiles…** at the bottom.

- **Photo:** the bot's photo in servers using the profile: the middle square of a PNG, JPEG, GIF (its first frame) or WebP image of up to 5 MB, made 512 pixels square. Discord lets a bot have its own photo in each server, so each server shows its profile's, and a profile without one shows the bot's own photo from the Discord Developer Portal, which never changes. It changes when a server picks the profile, when the profile's photo changes, and at start-up for a server where it didn't go through. If Discord turns a change down, the toast and Settings say why; if Discord makes the bot wait, it changes by itself once it can.
- **Nickname:** what the bot is called in servers using the profile, up to 32 characters. Empty keeps its own name: switching from a profile with a nickname to one without puts the bot's own name back, while a nickname an admin gave it by hand stays until a profile with one is picked. Renaming needs **Change Nickname** for the bot's role, which @everyone has unless a server took it away; Settings says so when it's missing.

- **Personality:** who it is, in a few sentences, up to 1000 characters. 300 to 900 works best: enough for a voice of its own, without rules that fight the bot's.
- **Answer length:** **Medium** (up to three sentences, the default), **Short** (one or two) or **Chatty** (a short paragraph), unless someone asks for more.
- **Opinions:** **Straight**, the default, gives real opinions without sitting on the fence; **Diplomatic** gives them tactfully, allowing that others see it differently.
- **Swearing:** **When it fits** its character (the default), **Never**, even when people swear at it, or **Freely**.
- **Roasts:** how hard it hits back when [Roast rude people](#settings) is on: **Savage** (up to three sentences, no softening, the default), **Cutting** (one insult, two sentences at most) or **Playful** (teasing, more ribbing than wounding).
- **Can't stand:** who it tears into on sight when someone sides with them or asks about them, for what they stand for and never for who they are. It starts as "fascists, Nazis and those who cheer them on"; empty is nobody.
- **Voice** and **Effect:** what it says its own lines in: answers in voice, hop-on and game night announcements, and greetings. **The server's default** follows the server's [default voice and effect](speech.md). /say, messages read out and the Soundboard's say box keep the server's default either way. **Hear it** plays the voice and effect picked.

Whatever the profile, it never goes after someone's looks, family, race, religion, gender, sexuality or health, and it doesn't assume anyone's gender.

The **Robot butler** is built in. It can be changed, and given a photo, and **Reset** puts it back without one; it can't be deleted. Deleting another profile puts the servers that used it back on the Robot butler, and removing a voice puts the profiles that used it on the server's default voice. A server that had changed its personality before there were profiles gets a profile of it, named from its first words, with the default voice and effect it had, and keeps talking and sounding as it did.

## Privacy and cost

Each answer sends the message, the channel's last 20 messages (with people's display names), images attached to the message or the one it replies to, what's playing, who's in voice channels everyone can see, the games people are playing, the server's commands, what it looks up for that answer (like people's games, play time, deals or songs played), the names of the people it can ping, the asker's note if they have one, and the server's name to [Anthropic's API](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms). With lines on automatic posts, each post's text goes too. Catching someone up sends up to 500 of that channel's messages from the last 3 days, about a cent with Haiku. A web search sends what the model searches for, not who asked, to Anthropic's search provider. Nothing is sent for messages that don't @mention the bot or reply to one of its answers, so tell people in the server what @mentioning it does. The bot's log notes each thing it does in chat (the action, what it was given and what came back), for working out what went wrong. Reminders stay in the bot's database only until they're posted or cancelled.

With Haiku, an answer costs about a twentieth of a cent while people are chatting with it, and up to a tenth of a cent when answers are spread out, as its instructions are kept ready for 5 minutes at a time: roughly $1.50–3 a month at 100 answers a day. With Sonnet it's about 1 cent, or roughly $20–60 a month, and with Opus about 2–4 cents, or roughly $60–120 a month at that rate. An image adds up to a twentieth of a cent with Haiku, and up to about 2 cents with Opus. A web search costs 1 cent, plus the pages it reads, about 15,000 tokens: an answer that searches is about 1.2 cents with Haiku, 4 cents with Sonnet and 7 cents with Opus. Anthropic takes about 20 seconds to get ready for chat's tools the first time it sees them, and again once they've gone unused for a day, and the answer that finds them so waits that long. So the bot sends each server's tools in a request for a single token when it starts, and when people get going (someone joins voice or posts) after its tools haven't been used for 20 hours: about a tenth of a cent each time with Haiku, 2 cents with Sonnet and 4 with Opus. The Chat card shows what it has actually cost.

## Setting it up

1. Make an account at [console.anthropic.com](https://console.anthropic.com), add some credit, and create an API key.
2. Set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` on the bot (see [Self-hosting](self-hosting.md#configuration)) and restart it. Music needs the [music container](self-hosting.md#the-music-container), which finds things by name on YouTube Music, or on Spotify with a Spotify app, and speech the [voice container](self-hosting.md#the-voice-container).
3. The bot answers in channels where it has **Send Messages** and **Read Message History** (an answer is a reply, and it reads what came before). Private channels mentioned in a message are sent as "a channel", not by name.
